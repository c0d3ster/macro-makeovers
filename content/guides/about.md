**Most comfort food blows the fat budget without buying you much protein for it.** A real Alfredo can run 50g+ fat for barely more protein than plain pasta. The other direction has the opposite problem: a lot of "diet" or "healthy" recipes hit the macros by stripping the dish down until it stops tasting like the thing it's named after. _Neither one is actually livable long-term: one wrecks the numbers, the other wrecks the food._

**The bet here is that you don't have to choose.** Every recipe here starts from a dish worth eating and gets rebuilt ingredient by ingredient: swap the fat-heavy component for a leaner base, then spend real effort putting the flavor back with aromatics, spice, and technique, instead of just serving a smaller, sadder portion of the original. The "traditional" row in every nutrition table is the receipt: same dish, real numbers, before and after.

**One recipe, more than one way to eat it.** Many recipes come in builds: the same dish as a griller or a rice bowl, with beef in place of turkey, or with a store-bought shortcut. Each build has its own row in the nutrition table, and picking one rewrites the ingredients and steps to match, so the numbers always describe what you're cooking.
