Each recipe heading reads like `$$ · ~40 min · ●●●○○`: cost, time, and difficulty. A spicy recipe adds its heat in chilis, like `🌶🌶`.

### Cost

Cost is per serving, from the lowest prices found across several stores for each ingredient, so it is a relative guide and prices will differ where you shop.

- **$:** under $3
- **$$:** $3 to $4.99
- **$$$:** $5 to $6.99
- **$$$$:** $7 to $9.99
- **$$$$$:** $10 and up. No recipe reaches it yet.

### Time

Time is hands-on prep plus cooking. A wait like a marinade is listed beside it, as in "~60 min + 2 hr marinade".

### Difficulty

Difficulty is about technique risk, not effort. A long recipe with forgiving steps stays low. A short one where a small mistake ruins the dish goes high.

- **1 ●○○○○:** assembly, almost no cooking.
- **2 ●●○○○:** one forgiving method, hard to mess up.
- **3 ●●●○○:** a few components, or one spot to watch.
- **4 ●●●●○:** several things to time together, or one technique that can fail.
- **5 ●●●●●:** a technique where a small mistake ruins it (poached eggs and hollandaise, a thick steak, a soufflé). No recipe is a 5 yet.

### Heat

Heat is rated for a typical eater, and only spicy recipes get chilis. Most recipes have a dial for more, like jalapeño seeds or extra cayenne, and the rating is for the amounts as written.

- **1 🌶:** mild. A little warmth most people won't notice.
- **2 🌶🌶:** medium. Clearly spicy, like a jalapeño-forward dish.
- **3 🌶🌶🌶:** hot. Real heat from habanero or a heavy hand of cayenne.
- **4 🌶🌶🌶🌶:** very hot. For people who go looking for heat.
- **5 🌶🌶🌶🌶🌶:** extra hot. The top of an Indian restaurant's scale.

### Nutrition

Every nutrition table is per serving and estimated. Ratios read carbs / fat / protein (C/F/P) as shares of calories. The italic traditional row is the same dish made the usual way, so you can see what the makeover saves.

### Make-or-break, fixes, and levers

- **Make-or-break:** the few steps that decide whether the dish works. If you only read one list before cooking, read this one.
- **Fixes:** what to do when something goes wrong, such as a sauce that turns grainy or too thin.
- **Levers:** changes you can make to hit a different goal, like more cheese for fat, fewer tortillas or less rice for carbs, or more meat for protein. Where the numbers move, the table shows by how much.
- **Options:** some recipes compare smaller swaps in a table, like the meat options in the enchilada casserole. A swap worth cooking on its own gets a build.

### Builds

Many recipes come in more than one build: the same dish made a different way. The taco grillers can use 90/10 beef in place of turkey, or go over rice as a bowl. The pollo asado bowl can start from Costco's precooked chicken. Each build is its own row in the nutrition table, with its own macros, and keeps the recipe's method, changing only the lines and steps that differ.

- **On the site:** tap a row in the nutrition table to switch builds. The title, ingredients, steps, and notes change to match, and lines only that build uses are marked "This build". The link keeps your pick, so you can save or share it.
- **In the book:** the table lists every build, and a line or step only some builds use says which, like "(Lean beef grillers only)".
- **Browsing:** with a goal or filter set, each recipe card leads with the build that fits best. Filter for beef and the turkey tacos show up as Lean Beef Taco Grillers.
- **In the basket:** add the build you plan to cook, and the shopping list and price follow it.
