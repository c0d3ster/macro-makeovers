## Where the numbers come from

Each recipe's calories, carbs, fat, and protein are worked out from its ingredients and amounts, per serving, using typical nutrition data for those ingredients. Calories carry a "~" because they are rounded. The traditional row in each table is a typical version of the same dish, not a specific restaurant or brand.

## Why yours will differ

- Brands and cuts vary. Two packs of "90/10 ground beef" or "Greek yogurt" can differ by a lot, so check the labels on what you buy.
- Portions vary. A serving is the batch split evenly, so a heavier scoop has more of everything.
- Cooking changes weight. Fat renders out, water cooks off, and oil stays in the pan or soaks in.
- Swaps change the math. Skipping, adding, or replacing an ingredient means the table no longer describes your plate.

The same goes for the C/F/P ratios, the whole-batch totals, preview images, the book, and anything else that shows a total. Protein, fat, and carb grams are all estimates in the same way.

## Not medical advice

{{brand}} shares recipes and the general thinking behind them. It is not a doctor, a registered dietitian, or a treatment plan. The protein and calorie targets in the guides are general ranges for healthy adults, not a prescription for you.

Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before changing how you eat if you are pregnant or nursing, under 18, have kidney or liver disease, diabetes, or another medical condition, take medication affected by diet, or have a history of disordered eating. If something you eat makes you feel unwell, stop and get medical help.

## Allergies and food safety

Recipes may contain common allergens, including milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, wheat, soy, tree nuts, and peanuts, and store-bought ingredients can contain more than their names suggest. Read every label if you have an allergy. Cook meat, poultry, fish, and eggs to safe temperatures, and store leftovers safely.

## No tracking

{{brand}} does not log what you eat or track your daily intake. If you use a tracking app, treat the numbers here as a starting point and adjust to your own ingredients.
