# Meal planning and meal kit market

Research for the paid plan and the basket. Checked 2026-11-08. App Store ratings come from Apple's public lookup API on that date. Anything marked **unverified** comes from third-party sites only. Market sizes from paywalled research firms are marked **paywalled estimate**, and they disagree by large factors. Grocery handoff options are in [`grocery-services.md`](./grocery-services.md).

## Summary

- **The opening is real.** About 70% of Americans say they try to eat more protein, high-protein has been the most-followed eating pattern three years running, and about 12% of adults are on a GLP-1 drug. None of the meal planners with grocery handoff lead on macros. The macro apps (MacroFactor, RP, Carbon) don't plan meals or shop. Eat This Much and MyFitnessPal Premium+ come closest.
- **Price is set.** Planning subscriptions cluster at $5 to $7 a month, or about $50 to $60 a year (eMeals, Plan to Eat, Samsung Food+, Eat This Much). Macro coaching apps charge $12 to $25 a month. A $5 plan is at market. An annual price near $49 to $60 is the norm.
- **Grocery handoff is common and rarely the moat.** eMeals leads on retailer breadth (Walmart, Kroger, Amazon, Instacart, Albertsons, Safeway). Most others use Instacart only, or nothing.
- **The field is consolidating.** Mealime shut down on 2026-10-21, and PlateJoy closed earlier. HelloFresh revenue fell 9% in 2025. Hungryroot grew 55% to $700M as a personalized grocery service, not a classic meal kit.
- **The gap to take:** honest in-store cost per serving plus macros, with real carts at Walmart and Kroger. Nobody in this list does all three.

**What reviewers say.**

- **eMeals:** praised for the Walmart integration ("saved me hours each week"). Complaints are billing: auto-renewal and refunds denied after trials (Trustpilot).
- **Cooklist:** store links expire every few days.
- **Others:** I found little primary review data. The roundups are mostly SEO content (unverified).

**What sits behind the paywall:**

- **Grocery handoff:** paid at eMeals, since the whole service is paid. Free or promo-led at MyFitnessPal and Samsung Food, as a perk of the paid tier.
- **Macro targets:** paid at MacroFactor, RP and Carbon. A mix of free and Premium at Eat This Much.
- **Planning itself:** free at Samsung Food, paid at Plan to Eat and eMeals.

**How they earn from grocery.** None publish it. eMeals and MyFitnessPal likely earn Instacart partner revenue (unverified). Walmart and Kroger integrations at eMeals look like partnerships with unknown terms (unverified).

## eMeals in depth

eMeals has the most grocery handoffs in this list, so it is the closest model for "Send to cart". Checked 2026-11-09.

**Company.** Based in Birmingham, AL. It bought RecipeBox (a free recipe app with about 200k users) in April 2022, and hired two former Shipt executives in 2021 to lead growth. Revenue of about $14.7M appears only on data-broker sites (**unverified**). No subscriber count is public.

**Product.** Users pick a themed plan (Clean Eating, Keto, Low Carb, Low Calorie, Diabetic, Kid Friendly and others) and get a weekly dinner menu plus a shopping list. About $5 a month billed yearly, no free tier after the trial. Walmart+ runs promos with it (60-day trial plus $10 Walmart Cash), but eMeals is not a Walmart+ benefit.

**How the cart handoff works.**

- **Walmart:** the user links their Walmart account. eMeals matches each list item to an in-stock product at the local store, the user can swap items, then one tap fills the cart. A third-party test moved a 4-dinner list to Walmart pickup with no fixes needed. This is the polished path.
- **Kroger:** per eMeals' own help page, ingredients are added one at a time with a "Next" button. Reviews report an empty cart after sending, and a Kroger view inside the app that is separate from the real Kroger app.
- **Others:** Instacart, Shipt, Amazon Fresh, Albertsons and Safeway (added 2020), H-E-B, chosen by zip code. The "90% of US zip codes" claim dates from 2018.
- **Terms:** the partnership terms and revenue are not public.

**Nutrition.** Plan-level, not personal. eMeals' support page says nutrition info covers only Keto, Low Calorie, Low Carb, Clean Eating, Quick and Healthy, Diabetic and Heart Healthy. For other plans, support offers a link to drop recipes into MyFitnessPal. Targets are simple caps: under 500 calories per serving (Low Calorie, Diabetic), 30 to 55g carbs (Diabetic), under 20g net carbs (Low Carb). No protein targets, macro ratios or user goals found.

**Complaints.** Auto-renewal and refunds denied after trials, limited meal swaps, repetitive recipes, plans that don't suit picky eaters, portions that can't be adjusted, and Kroger handoff glitches. Praise centers on convenience and the Walmart handoff.

**What "eMeals with macros" looks like.**

| eMeals | Macro Makeovers |
| --- | --- |
| Pick a diet plan | Pick a goal (fat loss, bulk, maintain) and get macro targets |
| Calorie or carb caps on some plans | C/F/P and protein per serving on every recipe |
| Fixed weekly menu, few swaps | Choose or swap recipes that fit the goal, scaled to servings |
| No price until checkout | In-store cost per serving and trip cost up front |
| One-tap Walmart, clunky Kroger | One-tap Walmart and a real Kroger API cart |
| Billing complaints | Clear trial, easy cancel, reminder before renewal |

**Takeaways.**

- Their breadth comes from years of partnerships, but only Walmart is polished. Our Walmart URL plus Kroger API plan (see [`grocery-services.md`](./grocery-services.md)) matches their best path and can beat them on Kroger.
- Personal targets and goal scoring are the moat. eMeals could add a macro filter, but rebuilding themed plans around personal goals is harder. That makes PRD 02 the priority.
- Recipe count is the biggest gap. About 15 recipes can't carry weekly planning. Grow the library, or position the paid plan as macro-fit makeovers rather than every dinner, every week.
- Up-front cost only stays an advantage if checkout lands close to the estimate, so keep pickup at no-markup grocers as the default.

**Fit for a "Macro Makeovers box."** The precedents are celebrity or large-creator recipe series run by the kit company (Green Chef with Emily Mariko, HelloFresh with Chef Tini). ## Market data

**Online grocery (Brick Meets Click):**
- Q3 2026: US online grocery sales rose 20.7% year over year to a record 20.9% of total grocery spending. Delivery grew over 30%, pickup grew mid single digits, and ship-to-home grew a little slower than the market. 17% of monthly users used all three methods in the same month.
- Q1 2026: sales rose 19%, to more than 19% of grocery sales.
- Split by method, May 2025 (latest monthly figure found): delivery 45.4%, pickup 37.2%, ship-to-home 17.5%. Delivery is gaining fast and pickup is losing share, so a pickup-first default fits honest costs but goes against the trend.

**Meal kits:**
- HelloFresh 2025 revenue fell 9% in constant currency, with 2026 guided at -6% to -3%. Ready-to-eat (Factor) and personalized grocery (Hungryroot) are where the growth is.
- Global meal kit market about $21.1B in 2025, North America 42% (Fortune Business Insights, paywalled estimate).

**Meal planning apps:**
- US about $566M in 2025, rising to $1.53B by 2035 (SNS Insider, paywalled estimate).
- Global 2025 to 2026 estimates range from $390M to $3B across firms (paywalled estimates).

**Subscription benchmarks:**
- Health and fitness trial-to-paid: median 39.9%, top decile 68.3% (RevenueCat 2025). 35.0% (Adapty 2026).
- Health and fitness leads revenue per install: $0.48 at day 14, $0.66 at day 60 (RevenueCat 2026).
- Annual plans renew at about 40% across all categories. About 30% of annual subscribers cancel in the first month (RevenueCat). I found no health and fitness figure for 12-month renewal.
- Trials of 4 days or less convert at 26.8%, so use 7 to 14 days.

**High-protein and macro audience:**
- 70 to 71% of Americans tried to consume protein in 2025. High-protein was the most-followed diet for the third year. 35% raised their protein intake in the past year (IFIC 2025).
- 12% of US adults currently take a GLP-1 drug (KFF, November 2025, up from 6% in May 2024). Gallup's mid-2026 figure is 11% taking them for weight loss.
- Macro-tracking app scale: MyFitnessPal has 2.39M App Store ratings. MacroFactor, RP and Carbon each have 8k to 24k.

## Gaps and openings for Macro Makeovers

1. **Honest cost plus macros.** No competitor shows an in-store cost per serving next to macros. Keep the cost estimate front and center, and make pickup at a no-markup grocer the default.
2. **Comfort food, made over.** Macro apps give targets, not food people want to eat. Meal planners give food without macros. The makeovers sit between the two.
3. **Real carts without Instacart.** Only eMeals offers Walmart and Kroger together, and it has billing complaints and no macro focus.
4. **GLP-1 and protein demand.** High-protein, smaller portions and protein per serving fit this audience. Mention it in copy without making medical claims.
5. **Gaps to close:**
   - **Recipe count:** about 15 recipes against thousands at eMeals. Meal planning needs variety to retain paying users.
   - **Macro targets:** no personal targets yet (PRD 02 covers this).
   - **No native app:** competitors are app-first.
   - **No reviews yet:** there's no social proof.
6. **Retention risk.** Annual plans renew at about 40%, and a small library hurts retention. Grow the recipe count before pushing annual plans hard. Offer a 7 to 14 day trial.
