## Method and assumptions

Each ingredient is costed as pack price divided by pack quantity, times the amount the recipe uses. The batch total is the sum, and per serving is the batch divided by the recipe's serving count. Cost columns are rounded to the cent, so line items can differ from the batch total by 1 to 2 cents. The full-pack cost is what it takes to buy everything in the recipe from scratch, rounded up to whole packs, with exact weight for items sold per pound. It is split into shopping items and pantry staples. Pantry staples are oils, butter, milk, flour, cornstarch, brown sugar, soy sauce, Worcestershire, sesame oil and seeds, and every spice, since most kitchens already hold them. Every item is priced at a small pack close to the recipe amount, so neither the prorated nor the full-pack figures lean on bulk sizes.

- **Leftover components are prorated by use.** The taco pico (4/7 of a batch), the pollo asado salsa (4/5 of a batch) and the cottage cheese blend (5/6 of a batch in the chili, 2/3 in the tacos) are costed only for the share the recipe consumes.
- **Spices are costed per teaspoon.** Jar price divided by the teaspoons in the jar, using ounces per jar and grams per teaspoon.
- **Whole items are priced each and prorated by the fraction used.** Half an onion costs half the price of one.
- **Each ingredient uses the lowest price found.** Prices come from the Martin's shelf at the Winchester, VA store (read October 29), your Martin's receipt, and Walmart, Kroger, HEB, ShopRite, Acme and Costco listings. The price book shows the winning store and the other prices found for every item that has more than one. Items not yet read at Martin's (produce, spices, sesame seeds, orange juice, achiote, adobo, sugars, vinegar, basil, pickles, flank and black rice) carry their earlier retail or receipt price. Treat the totals as relative costs, since prices vary by region and store.
- **Pack sizes and approximations.** Oils are priced at the smallest bottles found (8.4 fl oz avocado, 17 fl oz olive). Cheddar is Tillamook Extra Sharp White at HEB's 16 oz price, not store brand. Chicken breast is your $3.00 per lb target. The baby potato bag is assumed to be 1.5 lb.
- **Not costed.** Salt, water, and the optional cocoa or chocolate in the chili.
